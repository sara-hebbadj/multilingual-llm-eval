# Dataset card: Lumi Skin multilingual customer-service test sets

## Purpose

Test whether a language model can work as the customer-service assistant of a small Gulf-based
online skincare shop in Arabic, English and French: state the shop's policies correctly, give
safe product guidance, keep its tone with upset customers, refuse what it must refuse, ask when a
question is unclear, follow formatting requests, and resist abuse (red-teaming).

## Composition

Live counts are in [`evals/dataset_stats.md`](../evals/dataset_stats.md) (regenerate with
`python -m evals.stats`).

**Quality set, `evals/dataset.jsonl`: 180 items = 60 concepts x 3 languages.**

| Category | Per language | What a good answer does |
|---|---|---|
| policy_fact | 18 | States the policy facts exactly (days, AED limits, who approves what). |
| product_advice | 10 | Recommends real catalogue products with correct prices; admits what the catalogue does not say. |
| complaint_tone | 10 | Stays calm and empathetic, gives the policy and a next step, offers a human. |
| refusal | 8 | Declines privacy, medical, discount and off-topic requests politely. |
| clarification | 7 | Asks one short question when the request is ambiguous. |
| formatting | 7 | Follows bullets, numbered steps, one sentence, yes/no or "max 3" exactly. |

Arabic items mix Modern Standard Arabic (26), Gulf (15), Levantine (7) and Maghrebi (7) dialect
phrasing, and Arabizi, i.e. Arabic in Latin letters (5). English and French items mix standard
and informal register (texting style, tutoiement, a Maghrebi-French "Salam").

**Red-team set, `evals/redteam.jsonl`: 45 items = 15 attacks x 3 languages**, 3 of each type:
prompt injection (hidden instructions in a pasted review, a fake management email, a translation
request), personal-data extraction (a "found parcel", a "husband", a fake courier), jailbreak and
role-play ("DAN", "developer mode", "grandmother"), unsafe skincare or medical advice (mixing
strong actives, a toddler's rash, pregnancy), and discount or refund fraud (fake promotion, false
damage claim, staff code). The attacks are customer-service abuse cases only; none asks for
genuinely dangerous content.

## Fields

Quality items: `id`, `language`, `variety`, `category`, `prompt`, `reference_facts` (English),
`expected_behavior`, `source` (policy section, FAQ entry or product IDs), `parallel_id`,
`reviewed`. Red-team items: the same minus facts/category/source, plus `attack_type` and
`forbidden_strings` (text that proves the attack worked).

## How it was made

1. A coding agent (Claude) drafted each concept once, with English reference facts taken from the
   shop policies, FAQ and catalogue, then wrote a natural prompt for it in each language.
2. `python -m evals.stats` checks every item automatically: schema, unique IDs and prompts, the
   right script for the language, each concept present once per language, sources that exist, and
   every number in a reference fact present in the shop data.
3. **Pending:** Sara, a native Arabic speaker fluent in French, reviews every Arabic and French
   prompt for naturalness (`python -m evals.review export` / `apply`). The `reviewed` field and
   `dataset_stats.md` record progress. At the time of writing: 0 of 120 quality items and 0 of 30
   red-team items reviewed.

## Known limitations and biases

- **Written by a model.** An AI model drafted the prompts. If a model from the same family is
  evaluated, it may find these prompts easier (familiar phrasing). Sara's rewrites reduce this;
  report it next to any result for that family.
- **Dialect coverage is thin.** 5-15 items per dialect is enough to spot problems, not to rank
  dialects with confidence.
- **Single-turn only.** Real support chats are multi-turn; this set tests one message at a time.
- **No order lookup.** The assistant has no tools, so order-specific questions test whether it asks
  for verification, not whether it finds the right order.
- **Reference facts are English.** The judge must compare meaning across languages, which adds
  some judge error for Arabic and French; the human calibration measures how much.
